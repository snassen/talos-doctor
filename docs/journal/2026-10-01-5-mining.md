# 5. Mining rules from Jev's catches: a lesson in what the ordinary side must hold

**The idea.** Jev caught 9,899 attacks the rules missed. Phrasings common among them and absent from ordinary text
could become new rules: free, instant, explainable, and independent of Jev.

**The method** (in Talos's lab, `talos screen mine`). Samples split by a hash of their id, 70% to mine from, 30% held
back. From the mined part: the attacks the rules let through and Jev caught (6,975); their word pairs, triples and
quadruples, line by line (talos-doctor judges line by line) and normalized as the screen reads; kept when at least
15 of them have it and no ordinary sample does; chosen greedily, each adding the most attacks not yet covered.
Every candidate measured on the held-back part.

**First attempt: too good to be true.** 16 candidates would have caught 2,862 of the 3,858 held-back attacks the
rules miss, at 2 false alarms in 1,374 ordinary samples. Nearly every one came 100% from a single source: the fixed
address and subject that every LLMail-Inject attacker had to use, and that challenge's e-mail format. A dataset's
habit, not a sign of injection. Others ("email to", "follow up") would fire on ordinary e-mail all day; they only
looked clean because the ordinary side held 132 real e-mails.

**Second attempt: a phrasing must be spread over sources.** Its catches must come from at least two sources, none
with more than 80% of them. 15 candidates survived, together catching 131 of the 3,858 (3.4%) at 3 false alarms.
Most are common English ("right away", "so i can", "approved by"); even the most injection-like, "follow these
instructions", is in half the world's READMEs, which the ordinary side again lacks.

**Decision.** None accepted. The rules stay what they are good at: hidden and encoded text, structure, and a few
unmistakable phrasings, at 0.7% false alarms. Free wording is Jev's.

**What would make mining worth it.**

1. A far larger and broader ordinary side: public code and documentation from many repositories (READMEs above
   all), and ordinary e-mail at scale. A candidate can only be trusted as far as the ordinary text it was checked
   against reaches.
2. Combinations rather than single phrasings (a weak phrasing next to an address, a request and an urgency), which
   is a small scoring model rather than a rule, and is where a learned combination of Jev's answers comes in
   (next.md).
